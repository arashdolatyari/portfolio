Golet, AI plant care startup

Validating the problem statement for a startup

Seed investors asked an AI plant care startup to prove that its problem was real. I interviewed 50 plant owners, defined the main problem, and tested if people would pay to solve it.

The challenge

Golet started in early 2024 to build an AI app for houseplant care. When I joined, the team was getting ready to raise a seed round, and the investors had one condition: a validated problem statement.

We had two goals:

  • Find the problems people have with plant care, by interviewing at least 50 users
  • Prove that people are willing to pay to solve them

Finding 50 people to talk to

Finding plant owners to talk to was a design problem by itself. Each try taught me where to look next.

  1. Local flower shopsThe weather was bad, so not many people came in. Five interviews.
  2. University officesI went back to my university and visited staff who kept plants on their desks. When I said it was research for an essay, people were happy to help. About 20 interviews in total.
  3. Online plant forumsI spent a full day on outreach, and nobody signed up.
  4. Friends of friendsI asked my friends for contacts who had houseplants and got about 40 phone numbers. This was enough to reach the goal.

Making sense of the interviews

First, I removed the interviews where people had no real plant problem and no interest in paying for help. Then I read every remaining interview and tagged each problem, to see which ones came up the most.

The problem statement

After the analysis, the team met and agreed on one main problem for the MVP:

People don't know enough about how to care for their plants. This leads to growth problems or diseases, and they are willing to pay for a solution.

Testing willingness to pay

We chose two business models to test:

  • Pay as you goPay each time the AI identifies a plant disease.
  • SubscriptionA monthly plan with a set number of identifications.

Working with the limits we had

The development team couldn't add test pages to the app in time. So we used the blog instead. It had 700 to 1,000 visitors a day, and we added targeted campaigns to bring more traffic. I designed the campaign banner in Figma.

The test design

I designed a user flow and UI screens that let us test both models inside the blog, without changing the app.

Results

We ran the test for seven days and tracked it with Google Analytics.
