AI Home Design, Revive Design Lab, 2024

How real user behavior changed our product

We talked to 20 people who canceled an AI photo editing tool for real estate. They showed us why the product wasn't converting, so we rebuilt it around how professionals really used it.

My role

I was a product designer and worked with the product, marketing, and development teams. I:

  • Ran user interviews to learn how paying users worked with the platform and where they had problems
  • Redesigned the landing pages so people could find and understand the most useful tools faster
  • Worked with developers and marketing during the build to keep the design consistent

The project

AI Home Design helps real estate professionals edit property photos in seconds. It has tools for virtual staging, image enhancement, and fixes that make a photo ready to list.

How it started

We started a project to optimize the website for our users. To do that, we first had to know who our users were and how different groups used the product.

Our usage data showed that four tools made up 60% of all activity on the platform. We wanted to understand what stopped people from using these four tools. Our goal was simple: more people paying for a plan.

Our approach

This was the first time we did research on the product, so it was ground research. As a startup we had to move fast. We made a list of the questions we had and started inviting users to interviews.

We started with people who had bought a subscription. They were willing to pay, but the product didn't give them what they expected. These users were also the easiest to reach. We already had a flow that encouraged people to stay with the product, so we added a temporary step: to cancel their subscription, they had to do an interview with us.

We ran 20 semi-structured interviews around three questions:

  • Why did you start using the product?
  • What made you leave?
  • What is your role in real estate?

After the interviews, we summarized the findings and made changes to see the results. We didn't validate the findings first. With about 2,000 weekly users, a survey would have taken at least two weeks to give us reliable data. So we decided to move forward with what we had learned and measure the results live on the website.

What we found

Four problems came up again and again:

What users told us

  • Unclear pricingThey wanted to know the price per image before they decided.
  • No control over editsThey weren't sure they could edit the AI results, so they didn't want to commit.
  • Unclear advantage of our productPeople didn't know the product was made for real estate.
  • Hard to find the right toolThe tools they used most were mixed in with many others, so they struggled to find them.

More than one kind of customer

Realtors were not our only users. Photographers, agencies, and part-time realtors were paying too. Our focus was too narrow, so we expanded the research to check these other personas.

Our solutions

Information architecture based on real usage

We put every tool into clear categories. Virtual Staging, the most used tool, got its own category. The next three, Day to Dusk, Item Removal, and Image Enhancement, went into one group. Everything else moved under Renovation.

Positioning for real estate professionals

We changed the messaging from design language to the language real estate people use. The new hero line, “The Ultimate AI Toolbox for Property Listing,” told them the product was made for them, but it still welcomed other users.

Clear pricing and value

Now the landing pages show the exact price per photo and list the most requested features at the top.

A better research process

We added a short survey before each interview. When we knew each person's job in advance, it was easier to group participants and choose the right people to talk to.

Impact

Two weeks after launch, we measured the results. We used GA4 for the funnel and Metabase for tool usage and premium plans. Subscriptions per session went up 35%, signups per session went up 17%, and subscriptions per signup went up 20%.

A simpler structure and a clearer message helped people find the right tool and decide to pay for it.

What we learned

Focusing on one professional market worked. Improving the most used tools increased paid subscriptions. The next step was to add features that bring people back every day, to improve retention.
